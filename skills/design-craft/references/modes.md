# Screen modes

Derived from pbakaus/impeccable (Apache-2.0, see NOTICE) at commit

`skill/reference/mode-operate.md`, `mode-persuade.md` and `mode-read.md`. Modified by VTC:

are left out.

The mode names what the visitor's success looks like on one screen. Choose it per screen from
what the screen is for, write it in the screen's design contract, and let it set how far a
direction's world may reach into the screen.

## The four modes

- **Persuade**: the visitor decides and acts; the design is what they judge. Landing pages,
  marketing, pricing, sign-up. The first viewport makes the offer intelligible and desirable,
  shows the one action the category's visitors came to take in its working form (a search
  form, open slots, add to cart; never a decorative link to it elsewhere), and demonstrates
  something only this product can prove. The world may carry the page: its material is the
  ground and the major surfaces, its type sets the headlines. A world reduced to an accent on a
  neutral template is the category default with a sticker on it.
- **Operate**: the visitor completes a task. App screens, dashboards, editors, admin, settings.
  Someone who uses tools like this one knows where everything is before they notice the world.
  The world lends four things only: type, palette, density and one signature move. It never
  supplies the layout, the navigation model or the controls; every control is a standard one
  for the platform. Colour does real jobs: the shell, state colours, data series, selection and
  the signature move. A grey screen with one accent is the category default without its polish,
  not restraint.
- **Read**: the visitor understands something. Documentation, articles, guides, help,
  changelogs. The reader's question leads, and the document reads as its kind. The world owns
  the frame (masthead, navigation rail, ground, titles and section openers, how code and tables
  are set); the reading column stays calm: a reading face, real contrast, a measure of about 60
  to 75 characters, nothing performing behind the text.
- **Experience**: the visitor is inside the work. Portfolios, galleries, showcases. The work
  itself leads from the first viewport and the interface recedes.

## Directions per mode

- **Operate and Read**: candidate worlds come from anywhere in the audience's world (its graphic
  traditions, places, rituals, publications), never from the tools and instruments the audience
  operates. A terminal, a scope, a cockpit or a control panel handed to a working screen comes
  back as a costume of that tool: take from it only what transfers as type, palette, density and
  a signature move. Write each direction's first viewport as the product's shell first: the
  navigation, the work areas and what each is for, the data read first and its states, then
  where the signature move lives. Name the move concretely: service health drawn as a transit
  map, a schedule set in a timetable's column rhythm. A direction whose world shows up only as a
  colour scheme has not found its move yet.
- **Persuade and Experience**: the tools the audience operates count toward the rut; spend at
  most one candidate on them. The world may be the carrier of the page, never a picture of it:
  a viewport painted whole as a facade, a book cover or a poster, with no working parts in it,
  is a picture of the world, not its page. Navigation set into the world still reads as links,
  and the primary action is unmistakable.

## The self-check

Cover the product's name. An Operate screen must still read as a working product of its
category, with the task doable from it, at least as clean as the category standard and more
distinctive than it. A Read screen must still tell the reader where they are and where to go
next, with a body that is comfortable to read, and must not pass for any other product's
documentation. If a screen reads as an artifact of the world before it reads as its kind, the
world has reached too far: pull it back to type, palette, density and the one move.
