# New work: distinct directions in five steps

Derived from pbakaus/impeccable (Apache-2.0, see NOTICE) at commit
2f98774f86bc05563876643db37e6c37c322850f: `skill/reference/new-work.md` ("Create or replace the
visual world" steps 1–5, "Commit the world"). Modified by VTC: the upstream's roll script and
hosted catalog become the engine's constraints lottery (spec §9.1 step 2), its decision page
becomes gate G2 with 2–3 directions, verbalized sampling is added to step 2 (spec §9.1 step 1),
and challenger fusion is cut because no catalog deals challengers.

Use these steps only for a new product's visual world, or when the specification asks to
replace one. A screen, feature or state inside an established product inherits its world:
never turn a local addition into a new identity exercise.

## The steps

1. **Name the rut.** In one sentence each: the product's unique mechanism, the audience's real
   scene, its cultural home, and what the first screen must prove. Then write down the screen
   this category always ships and its predictable opposite. Both are the rut, and neither may
   be a candidate. A brief that paints its own picture (a product name, a governing metaphor)
   adds its literal reading to the rut: spend at most one candidate on it.
2. **List seven candidate worlds.** From the audience's culture, list seven concrete visual
   systems, artifacts, places or rituals the audience knows by heart: its graphic and screen
   traditions count, not only its objects. Give each one line on why it resonates and can carry
   the mechanism, and one on how it looks (its ground and main colours, its type voices, the one
   signature that makes it recognisable). Near-duplicates count once. When more than three share
   one material family, dig until the list spans at least three material families. The screen's mode
   limits where candidates may come from (`modes.md`). Verbalized sampling: give each candidate
   your estimate of how likely another designer is to land on it for this category, and drop
   the most probable ones before choosing; asking to be distinct does not escape the model's
   favourite answers, but naming and dropping them does.
3. **Turn the material into complete directions.** Each joins a reusable visual world (palette,
   materials, type voices, component language) to a concrete first viewport of the key screen:
   what is where, at what scale, and where the primary action sits.
4. **Take the roll.** The engine's dealt constraints (the `design constraints` line of the
   pack's Dispatch section: colour strategy, type pairing, density and grid, radius and
   elevation, motion, one signature interaction) are what keep every run from converging on the
   category default. No substitute and no skip: build every direction inside them. The world
   supplies the material, the constraints supply the system, the product supplies every fact,
   and clarity wins a conflict. When a world cannot carry the constraints, replace the world,
   never the constraints. On a re-roll, drop every direction already shown and any the novelty
   report names as too similar, and build from the newly dealt constraints.
5. **Present the directions.** Two or three, each fully committed and of equal weight: its
   world, its first viewport, its signature interaction and its honest risk (name its
   familiarity when that is true). Never rank them, never recommend one, never soften one
   toward the others. The human chooses at G2, where the engine also offers a re-roll and the
   standing exit: the category standard, played straight. That exit is the human's door, never
   yours: never recommend it or weigh a direction against it. Familiar and effective is a
   legitimate destination when the human picks it.

## Commit the world

- **Colour strategy before colours** (OKLCH): Restrained (neutrals tinted from the world plus
  one accent; common on Operate and Read screens, where colour still does real jobs), Committed
  (one saturated colour carries 30 to 60% of the surface), Full (three or four named roles) or
  Drenched (the surface is the colour). Persuade and Experience screens may take the bolder
  strategies. Colour commits at page scale: fields that own whole regions, not accents
  scattered over a neutral ground.
- **Dark or light** is never a default: write one sentence of physical scene (who uses this,
  where, under what light) and let it decide.
- **Faces** come from the subject's world, in the mode's register, and never from the font
  blocklist without a reason no other face could satisfy (the skill's core file).
- **Calibration** (the skill's core file): if someone could guess the look from the category
  alone, or from the category plus what it avoids, rework until neither answer is obvious. A
  bookish, warm or child-facing subject does not soften this: cream paper is the smallest corner
  of a book's world, and cream plus serif for a book subject is the default wearing the
  subject's clothes.
- **Negative constraints rule out devices, not energy.** "No gamification" rules out badges and
  streaks, not exuberance; adjectives about the product's behaviour (calm, quiet) do not set the
  screen's energy.

## Every direction must already be viable

Every relationship and claim it shows is true; it has a real palette and component family, a
distinctive composition with one product-specific experience, and it works at full-screen scale
with the available assets and within the performance budget. A candidate that fails on truth is
replaced before it is built, never rescued by the roll. Illustrative material may be authored at
full fidelity and labelled synthetic where a visitor could mistake it for the real thing; list
what must be replaced with real material.
